PULMONARY MECHANOBIOLOGY

Pulmonary epithelial injury during airway reopening

What can a moving air–liquid interface do to an epithelial monolayer, and can pretreatment change the observed injury under controlled in vitro conditions?

Peer-reviewed / First author / 2021Original research overview ↗

RESEARCH DOSSIER / EVIDENCE-LED RECORD

Research question, methods and contribution

An experimental study of pulmonary epithelial injury during the mechanical stresses associated with airway reopening.

Jump to sectionQuestionMethodsReadoutsContributionEvidence limitsPrimary source

The scientific question

Reopening a fluid-filled airway can expose its epithelium to a moving air–liquid interface. The question was whether pharmacological pretreatment could change susceptibility to that mechanical insult.

Approach and methods

L2 alveolar epithelial monolayers, parallel-plate flow chambers, air-bubble propagation, live/dead fluorescent assays, cytoskeletal microscopy and mechanical analysis.

MEASUREMENT / INTERPRETATION

How the evidence is read

Viability staining and cytoskeletal microscopy

What the measurement can establish

Fluorescent live/dead readouts can quantify staining-defined epithelial injury after controlled air–liquid interface exposure. Cytoskeletal microscopy adds spatial and morphological context under the experimental conditions; control exposures and consistent quantification remain essential.

What it does not establish

An altered fluorescence signal does not identify a unique molecular mechanism. A mechanically exposed L2-cell monolayer does not reproduce a complete human lung, and an in vitro reduction in injury cannot establish clinical protection.

My documented contribution

First-author research. Cultured and exposed epithelial monolayers, conducted flow-chamber studies, quantified fluorescence readouts, contributed mechanistic and statistical interpretation, prepared figures and drafted the paper.

Evidence, status and limitations

Published in Journal of Applied Physiology (2021). The reported comparison found substantially less cell death after dexamethasone pretreatment under the study conditions. In vitro evidence, not a patient-treatment claim.
