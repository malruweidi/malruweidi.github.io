Two Materials.
No Section.

An attempt to orient small zebrafish specimens ended not with interpretable tissue sections but with two embedding materials that separated under processing. The failure is documented. Its exact cause was not conclusively established.

03 / no invented explanation

What the record can establish.

The illustration of a mechanism must be answerable to the experiment, not the other way around.

There was an orientation problem, an attempted materials solution, and a visible failure during sectioning. The study did not produce reliable sections for interpreting zebrafish anatomy.

What was observed?

The 2% agarose support did not integrate into a mechanically coherent paraffin block under the attempted processing conditions. Separation and fragmentation interfered with microtomy. No interpretable histological section was obtained.

What explanation was considered?

The distinct hydrophilicity and processing behaviour of agarose and paraffin suggest a materials-interface problem. Agarose grade and thermal behaviour were among the suspected contributors. That possibility was not established as the sole cause; no follow-up experiment should be retroactively invented.

What could a future protocol investigate?

A properly documented comparison could examine embedding strategy, agarose concentration or grade, dehydration and clearing conditions, and the mechanical integrity of the final block. These are proposals for method development, not activities claimed to have been completed.

Source distinction: The three photographs document stages and objects associated with the attempt. The narrative is a first-person experimental recollection. The materials explanation is an interpretation with stated uncertainty; it is not a new publication or an authenticated protocol validation.
