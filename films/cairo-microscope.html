The microscope
I brought from Cairo.

I bought a microscope near Qasr El Einy, brought it to Qatar, and kept it in my room. One photograph from 2017 records something simple: being able to observe whenever I wanted.

02 / one photograph, accurately described

The original object.

The film is grounded in a personal account, not a claim of scientific discovery. The image documents the microscope in Qatar, not the shop, the border crossing, or any experimental result.

What brought it here?

In the original first-person story, I describe purchasing the microscope in Cairo’s Qasr El Einy area and bringing it to Qatar through a difficult journey. There was no recorded scientific experiment behind the photograph. It was a vacation morning when an instrument happened to be available for questions of my own.

The film briefly shows another archived university laboratory photograph to contrast institutional laboratory access with an instrument at home. It is identified as a separate internship photograph, not evidence of this microscope’s use or travel.

04 / archival and production notes

The photograph is not the journey.

The original photograph and independently identified contextual laboratory image are preserved as they were supplied. A typographic Cairo-to-Qatar line represents a first-person recollection, not footage or an independently documented route. The film makes no claim that a particular scientific specimen, measurement, or result was produced using the microscope at home.

Rendered with Python, Pillow and FFmpeg using original instrumental tones and image-based motion. No stock footage, synthetic laboratory scenes, synthetic speech or platform watermarks were used. Its reproducibility manifest records the SHA-256 digest of the video and archival photographs; the scene-by-scene source record remains accessible.
