The microscope
I brought from Cairo.

I bought my microscope in Cairo’s Qasr El Einy area and went through the trouble of bringing it to Qatar. In a photograph posted on 1 July 2017, it stands on my desk beside books, notes and an American Chemical Society mug. I could return to it without waiting for a scheduled laboratory session.

02 / the 2017 home microscope photograph

The microscope at home.

The photograph shows the instrument in Qatar, standing among books and notes. It was published in July 2017. The account of buying it in Cairo and carrying it to Qatar comes from my own recollection.

From Qasr El Einy.

The original first-person chapter recounts the purchase and the difficulties of transporting the instrument. In Qatar I had it within reach during a vacation morning, with no university experiment assigned to me. I could choose when to look through it, what to examine, and when to return to a question.

One scene also includes a photograph from a separate university laboratory internship. It provides context for the equipment and schedules I encountered there; it does not depict this microscope or its journey.

04 / archival and production notes

From Cairo to my desk.

The principal photograph appeared in a post dated 1 July 2017; its camera date has not been independently confirmed. The purchase in Qasr El Einy and the effort of transporting the microscope are my firsthand recollections. The Cairo-to-Qatar graphic represents that remembered journey. The university-laboratory photograph comes from a separate internship, and no particular specimen or published measurement is attributed to the home microscope.

Rendered with Python, Pillow and FFmpeg using original instrumental tones and image-based motion. No stock footage, synthetic laboratory scenes, synthetic speech or platform watermarks were used. Its reproducibility manifest records the SHA-256 digest of the video and archival photographs; the scene-by-scene source record remains accessible.
