Scientific figures answer specific questions under specific experimental conditions. A photomicrograph has a different evidentiary status from a schematic; an in-vitro readout does not become a clinical outcome; and a spectrum is meaningful only with its methods and controls. The published figures are not interchangeable with raw data. For the full experimental context, follow the primary-source links below.

01 / Pulmonary mechanobiology

A moving interface and an injured cell layer.

An experimental model asks how an air–liquid interface moving across an epithelial monolayer affects cellular viability under controlled conditions.

What this figure can help establish

The apparatus geometry and associated reported readouts provide context for experimentally imposed air–liquid interface exposure. Viability staining and other measurements must be interpreted with the paper's controls and quantification.

What this figure cannot establish

The image by itself cannot isolate a unique molecular mechanism, demonstrate therapeutic efficacy in patients, or substitute for an intact human airway. An in-vitro reduction in injury is not a clinical outcome.

02 / Developing vessels

A vessel develops inside an organism.

Developing cerebral vessels are influenced by biological context and hemodynamic conditions. Endothelial cilia and the flow environment are part of the question.

What this figure can help establish

Within the study's staged, imaged zebrafish specimens, anatomical and phenotypic observations can be related to the reported experimental groups, imaging conditions and perturbations.

What this figure cannot establish

A single microscopy image cannot demonstrate causal contributions from blood flow or cilia without the associated perturbation evidence, nor can a developmental phenotype be directly generalized to adult human disease.

03 / Polymer interfaces

The surface is part of the material.

Scanning electron microscopy provides morphological observations that help characterize a manufactured or treated polymer interface.

What this figure can help establish

Under the reported acquisition conditions, the micrograph may show morphological differences associated with sample processing or surface modification, subject to representative sampling and proper scale interpretation.

What this figure cannot establish

Appearance alone does not establish chemical composition, mechanical performance, antimicrobial efficacy or biocompatibility. Those claims require corresponding measurements and appropriate controls in the primary study.

04 / Molecular sensing

An optical response needs a chemical explanation.

A colour or absorption change is an observation under stated solution conditions. The chemistry becomes a stronger argument when spectral assignment, comparison experiments and analytical limits are included.

What this figure can help establish

With the experimental methods and controls, the spectral and colourimetric responses can support the reported sensing behaviour of the investigated imine compounds. Interpretation must follow the particular solvent, analyte and concentration conditions.

What this figure cannot establish

The figure does not by itself prove a unique binding mechanism or imply equivalent selectivity in every biological or environmental matrix. Nor does the optical response mean the initially proposed imidazopyridine synthesis succeeded.

Captions identify evidence boundaries, while original figure legends, scientific methods and publication rights must be checked in the primary sources for subsequent republication.
