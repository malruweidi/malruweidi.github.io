What Survives the Experiment / 02:00

The complete spoken text

The twelve paragraphs below reproduce the film's English narration. They form a personal research account, not twelve independent experimental conclusions. Watch the film or read its timed captions.

01 / The anatomy roomMy first anatomy lesson was not a drawing. A fox lay on the table. I wanted to understand how its parts worked together.

02 / A microscope of my ownLater, I brought a microscope from Cairo to Qatar. At home, curiosity did not have to wait for laboratory hours.

03 / Beginning with matterWe began with plant extracts and questions about medicinal chemistry. Some experiments were simple. The answers were rarely as simple.

04 / What a spectrum can sayImine synthesis and molecular sensing taught me to distrust a beautiful explanation until I could interrogate the spectrum.

05 / Learning in companyWe built a student chemistry community. Science grew in shared laboratories, long arguments, and the people who challenged our assumptions.

06 / When the polymer resistedA polymer would not behave as planned. I learned how little a plausible structure tells us about a real material.

07 / A living system movesThen I began following developing vessels in zebrafish. The organism moved, blood flowed, and measurement became a different problem.

08 / The section that failedAn embedding attempt failed when agarose and paraffin would not hold together. The microtome exposed a materials problem, not a usable section.

09 / An airway reopensIn the lung, we studied what happens when an airway reopens and a moving interface injures epithelial cells.

10 / The interface mattersBiomaterials and engineered surfaces added another constraint: a substance can be chemically interesting yet behave very differently at an interface.

11 / Reaching the lungNow I study dexamethasone vesicles intended for inhalation. A carrier must withstand nebulization before it can even be assessed for delivery.

12 / What can be relied upon?I also study how earlier pharmacists made medicines dependable. Across centuries and disciplines, I keep returning to what the evidence can actually bear.
