RECOMMENDED READING / BOOKS

Five books, different worlds.

From a fictional chemist to an Andalusian philosophical tale, these books invite questions about knowledge, autonomy, attention and the way we live.

Reading note

Fiction, philosophical allegory and popular nonfiction make different kinds of arguments. The notes distinguish what each work can reasonably support.

THE BOOKS / 05

The books

Brief reasons to read each title, with links to official publishers and one reservation about its claims or genre.

01 / BOOK
Fiction / science and society

Lessons in Chemistry

Bonnie Garmus · 2022

A novel about Elizabeth Zott, a chemist in 1960s America whose career encounters sexism and institutional resistance. It is especially interesting for the way it turns scientific curiosity and methodological stubbornness into a larger story about autonomy, work and public communication.

Read at the publisher ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Read it as fiction about the social life of science, not as an archival reconstruction of laboratory history.

A novel, not a historical source.
02 / BOOK
Psychology / reasoning

You Are Not So Smart

David McRaney · 2011

A lively introduction to cognitive biases, confabulation and the stories people use to explain their own decisions. The value lies in becoming more suspicious of effortless certainty, particularly when we are assessing evidence, remembering events or judging other people.

Read at the publisher ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

A useful starting point for studying self-deception, but individual chapters should not substitute for the underlying psychological literature.

Popular-science synthesis; examine the cited experiments independently.
03 / BOOK
Attention / scholarship

Deep Work

Cal Newport · 2016

Newport argues for protecting long intervals of undistracted concentration when the work requires learning, analysis or original thought. The book is useful for considering how research time is structured and how easy it is to confuse administrative motion with intellectual progress.

Read at the publisher ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Protected attention is valuable; it should not become a doctrine that dismisses collaboration, teaching or the unpredictability of experiments.

A practical argument about knowledge work, not a universal measure of productivity.
04 / BOOK
Psychology / focused experience

Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience

Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi · 1990

A foundational account of absorbing activity, attention and the relationship between perceived challenges and skills. It provides language for experiences familiar to people who spend hours at a bench, inside a text or working through a difficult problem.

Read at the publisher ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Flow describes a kind of experience; it does not by itself guarantee accurate science, a worthwhile objective or sustainable working conditions.

Original book published in 1990; linked publisher page is a later edition.
05 / BOOK
Medieval Arabic philosophy / knowledge

حي بن يقظان — Hayy ibn Yaqzan

Ibn Tufayl · 12th century · English edition translated by Lenn E. Goodman

A philosophical narrative of a child raised on an island who develops an understanding of nature, existence and society through independent observation and reason. The work connects medieval Arabic philosophy with questions about learning, empirical inference and the relationship between personal insight and inherited tradition.

Explore the translated edition ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Far more than an early tale of self-education, it is a sophisticated exercise in epistemology and the cultural history of inquiry.

The original is a medieval Arabic text. The linked University of Chicago Press edition (2009) is a modern English translation with an introduction and notes.
