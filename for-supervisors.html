Research profile / For prospective doctoral supervisors and collaborators

From the bench towards the bedside.

I am a chemistry graduate and MSc researcher in pharmaceutical sciences, interested in how molecules become useful medicines. My experimental path has moved from chemical sensing and polymeric materials to vascular biology, mechanically injured pulmonary cells and inhalable formulation. I now want to study therapeutic discovery alongside the chemistry, engineering and pharmacology that determine whether an intervention works in a living system.

My prospective doctoral direction is translational therapeutic engineering: connect a defensible target hypothesis to a designed intervention, its delivery, target engagement and a disease-relevant functional response. I am seeking depth in a focused project, not a laboratory expected to pursue every modality.

02 / Current work

What I am working on now.

My MSc research at Qatar University concerns dexamethasone-loaded niosomes and proniosomes intended for pulmonary delivery. The investigation involves formulation design, physicochemical characterization, nebulization and aerosol-performance questions. A stable carrier in a vial is not necessarily a useful carrier after administration; aerosol behavior, tissue exposure and biological activity must be established separately.

This project is ongoing. I do not present proposed or incomplete outcomes as demonstrated efficacy. My academic history also includes imine chemical sensing, polymeric and electrospun materials, cell-based mechanobiology, imaging and quantitative scientific workflows.

03 / Direction

Two connected doctoral starting points.

I am especially interested in chemical biology, pharmacology and engineered delivery. These are proposed directions for doctoral training, not claims that I have already synthesized PROTACs or built an organ-on-chip device.

Target engagement through chemical design.

Choose one disease-relevant protein state and test whether a carefully designed intervention changes its function. I want to learn and use bioorthogonal click chemistry and bioconjugation, bifunctional degraders such as PROTACs, molecular glues and aptamer-based recognition where the biology justifies each tool. Pair molecular characterization with target engagement, degradation or functional rescue, rather than relying on binding alone.

Delivery as a pharmacological experiment.

Choose a therapeutic cargo, such as an RNA modality, aptamer conjugate or targeted small molecule, and determine where productive exposure is lost. Develop lipid/polymeric or other appropriate carriers, investigate microfluidic formulation and transport, and measure intracellular fate and functional dose response in relevant cellular models. Connect formulation engineering to PK/PD and mechanistic predictions.

A microfluidic organ-on-chip or organoid model could then test a specific tissue barrier or disease state, but its predictive validity must be measured, not presumed. I want to connect molecular chemistry, engineering and quantitative pharmacology through experiments that could ultimately support translation from bench to bedside.

04 / Fit

Where I could contribute, and where I want to learn.

Existing foundation: experimental chemical and materials research, pulmonary epithelial injury, scientific imaging, formulation and aerosol questions, research writing, and collaborative computational work. My publication record and methods archive document these experiences with different degrees of individual involvement.

Doctoral training goals: advanced medicinal and click chemistry; PROTAC and other targeted-modality design; aptamer and RNA therapeutics; microfluidic manufacturing and organ-on-chip engineering; cellular target-engagement assays; experimental pharmacology, PK/PD and human-relevant disease modelling. These are priorities for developing deeper competence, not a list of techniques already mastered.

The common problem is practical: a chemically persuasive intervention is not a medicine until its exposure, action, selectivity and biological consequences can be demonstrated.
