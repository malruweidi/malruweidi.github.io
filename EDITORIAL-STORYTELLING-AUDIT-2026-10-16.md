# Ruwaidix — narrative, scientific-credibility and collaboration audit
**16 October 2026 · editorial working document · proposed branch, not live publication**

## Mandate and preservation rules
Make a stranger want to understand the researcher, and make a serious prospective collaborator know where to begin. The site is both a personal scientific history and an evidence-led scholarly record. Its approved aesthetic, routes, images, figures, videos, archival records, academic evidence and existing pages remain intact. No achievements, outcomes, dates, roles or affiliations may be invented to improve a story. The name of the MSc supervisor must not appear in the public account unless explicitly authorized.

**Scope:** Reviewed the current GitHub Pages source inventory (29 HTML routes, including the full-record, techniques and figures files accessible via their Git blobs), major narrative passages, project dossiers, metadata and page navigation, plus the bilingual translation mechanism. This is a source and editorial audit, not a claim to have rendered every page in every viewport, independently verified every external DOI or permission, or personally proofread every translated sentence. A separate live browser, accessibility and permission review remains appropriate.

## The decisive editorial observation
The site already has the material most researcher websites lack: actual objects, a visible chronology, difficult experiments, a research community, carefully bounded claims and first-person memories. Its weaker passages summarize these assets through an abstract research philosophy before allowing the reader to encounter them. Some explanatory disclaimers are necessary; when repeated in the hero, chapter, photograph and source box, they can sound like a rebuttal to a criticism the reader has not made. Keep full evidentiary qualifications in the connected research record and source notes. Give the story room to move.

The most compelling recurring motif is **the distance between the idea and what survives contact with matter or physiology**: a polymer that would not dissolve as expected; a hydrophilic agarose orientation block separating from paraffin; a moving air–liquid interface injuring epithelial cells; a vesicular formulation that must survive nebulization. This is memorable precisely because it is anchored in different real experiments rather than a generic interdisciplinary slogan. Historical scholarship about pharmacy can deepen the same concern with reliable preparation and substitution, without pretending that an historical manuscript is a modern laboratory trial.

## Editorial rules for all future additions
1. Open with the actual scene, object, observation or scientific problem where one exists. Specify what was physically attempted. Name materials, systems or methods when necessary for understanding.
2. Keep the first-person voice where the page is autobiographical. Use an archival or dossier voice for methods, publications and attribution. Do not force both registers into one paragraph.
3. Replace repeated generic reflections and formulaic contrasts with precise consequences: what changed in a method, decision, question or collaboration? Avoid theatrical one-sentence paragraphs, artificial triads and empty slogans.
4. Retain uncertainty at the level of the underlying claim. A failed experiment stays failed. An in vitro assay stays in vitro. A planned aerosol readout is not an achieved result. A photograph can document a moment without proving a mechanism.
5. Keep collaborative credit explicit. Founder or participant is not principal investigator; an institutional collaboration is not an individual employment claim; a certificate is not a peer-reviewed output.
6. Do not imply approval, affiliation, manuscript permissions, disease classification, image metadata or experimental results that the documents do not establish.
7. Make a collaboration invitation concrete: a research question, scientific system or historical corpus is a better opening than a long general list of interests.
8. Preserve bilingual equivalence. Existing Arabic translations are exact-key mappings; changed English copy requires new Arabic entries without deleting translations still used by legacy pages.

### Scientific direction and evidence
**research-horizon.html — Future questions.** The aspirations are serious but much of the language starts with 'I want'. Revised the lead to connect a stubborn polymer, controlled epithelial injury and current nebulization work to the question of predictive validity. Preserve future proposals as directions, not completed projects. A future second pass should specify decision criteria, relevant models, plausible negative controls and the first falsifiable comparison for each major proposed programme.

**research/pulmonary-epithelial-injury.html — First-author study.** Sound distinction between assay-derived injury, cytoskeletal imaging and clinical implications. Keep the L2 epithelial monolayer and air–liquid-interface geometry explicit, link to the published paper, and preserve the first-author contribution. Do not suggest a clinical treatment benefit from a controlled cell study.

**research/zebrafish-vascular-cilia.html — Collaborative developmental biology.** Collaborative role is appropriately attributed and the mechanistic scope is bounded. Keep species and developmental stage clear. Do not let a Ramadan memory function as primary data.

**research/inhalable-vesicles.html — Current MSc dossier.** Honest status separation between calculated formulation properties, actual preparation and planned chromatographic/DLS/NGI readouts is a major strength. The dossier should only acquire outcome figures when laboratory records support the methods, controls and statistics. Supervisor identity remains private. Standardize updates to planned versus completed endpoints as the project develops.

**techniques.html — Methods atlas.** Deeply valuable as proof of how work was done; large archival media dominate byte size. Lead with experimental problems over instrument inventories, retaining original recordings and attributed figures. Where a method is exposure/training rather than directly performed research, label it accurately. Avoid conflating diagnostic medical images with conceptual diagrams.

**figures.html — Primary research imagery.** Strong separation of original published microscopy, SEM, sensing data and conceptual schematics. Recheck publisher license, figure numbering, DOI, source and permission for each excerpt before broader syndication. The original images must not be represented as sole-author results in collaborative papers.

**imaging.html — Research infrastructure.** Previously opened in generic third person. Revised to the problem of turning a developing heart's motion into a defined measurement and then stated the documented imaging-workflow contribution. Preserve organizational credit and differentiate workflow development from establishing an entire independent unit, where documents do not support the stronger claim.

**computing.html — Computational research.** Keep the distinctive split between peer-reviewed co-first-author fetal echocardiography work and independent formulation software. Pilot cohort counts and absence of external clinical validation are vital to credibility. Demonstrate software value through source code, tests and documented workflows, not assertions of clinical readiness.

**grants/urep19.html — PAH micelles.** The thermodynamic hypothesis is distinctive. Do not convert planned micellization, partitioning or Flory–Huggins reasoning into accomplished measurements unless results exist. A later story could use an actual sample or notebook.

**grants/urep21.html — Pulmonary biomechanics.** Link the original undergraduate programme to subsequent peer-reviewed flow-chamber research without implying they are identical experiments or that all project milestones were achieved.

**grants/urep22.html — PLA scaffolds.** Distinguish the antimicrobial objective from measured antimicrobial performance and separate supported surface-chemistry publications from the original award aims.

**grants/urep23.html — Imidazopyridine plan and Schiff-base work.** Editorial opportunity of unusually high value: the final report describes a planned synthesis that did not yield the intended imidazopyridines, followed by a productive change of direction. This merits a fully developed first-person Stories chapter grounded in the final report. Preserve all scientific distinctions and avoid a triumphal 'failure led to success' narrative unsupported by experimental detail.

**stories.html — Main collection.** The most memorable content on the website. Revised the collection's first paragraph to name the school fox specimen, Cairo microscope, zebrafish nights and failed embedding block. Retain complete chapters and honest record notes. A subsequent storytelling pass should check paragraph by paragraph for explanation repeated both in the main narrative and in the adjacent evidence box.

**stories/cairo-microscope.html — Independent curiosity.** Replaced an unnecessarily self-disparaging comparison ('owning a camera') with what the instrument actually enabled: self-directed observation and sustained practice. **stories/failed-zebrafish-histology.html — Materials failure.** A model chapter: clear goal, chosen 2% agarose orientation medium, subsequent paraffin processing, observed separation and unsuccessful microtomy. Do not label the specimen as a successful histological result. Keep the proposed hydrogel–paraffin mechanism explicitly tentative.

**stories/unfortunate-copolymer-trials.html — Unsolved dissolution.** The notebook title provides wit without self-promotion; the distinction between apparent crosslinking and other causes of insolubility is scientifically necessary. **stories/ramadan-microscope.html — Science and obligations.** Human stakes are unusually clear: family guests waiting while the final experimental work continued. Keep the uncertainty about the precise year and preserve the difference between recollection and published vascular research. Avoid making the work sound heroic because social obligations were missed; the conflict itself is enough.

**archive.html — Images and life around the work.** Replaced passive catalogue language with a chronological invitation through school, lab benches, chemistry festivals, meetings and research visits. Keep every source image and its date-qualification. Extend captions with identified people and events only where the owner has established their identities.

**chem-squad.html — Seven scientists, long friendship.** Rewrote the opening around 2016 botanical-extract questions and ongoing weekly disagreements. Its human life should stay visible; future verified scenes (the tent and FIFA, a colleague asleep while someone photographed the room, or memories of the chemistry lab) belong here only with the right original image and owner-confirmed identification. Don't turn it into a corporate network page.

**engagement.html — Science in public.** Rewrote opening in a first-person, scene-led voice, keeping the fifth festival, student instrument training, school research judging and imaging work distinct. Preserve collective credit and contemporaneous poster records, including the 2018 poster versus later upload-label discrepancy.

**illness-and-evidence.html — Personal scientific history.** The emotionally difficult material benefits from a direct narrative while the diagnostic descriptions remain careful: eosinophilic GI involvement, historical labels and no implied final contemporary classification. The external autobiographical essay should be the main place for highly personal detail. A research collaborator should be able to read the laboratory narrative without being required to encounter medical history first.

Preserve the separate, verified Substack and Drug Discovery Online records.
