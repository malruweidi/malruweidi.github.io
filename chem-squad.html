01 / The beginning

Before the publications,
there was a laboratory.

In 2016, our organic chemistry conversations kept returning to myrrh and nutmeg. We wanted to understand how their constituents could be extracted and identified. The plants had long medicinal histories, but those histories could not tell us which compounds were present in a sample or whether an extract had biological activity.

We talked through the practical questions: solvent selection, separation, chromatography and the assays that would be needed before making a medicinal claim. Those discussions often continued after we had left the laboratory.

After graduation, we entered different research groups and professions. We continued sending each other papers, questioning methods and returning to ideas that had first come up in class.

We continued meeting after our original laboratory coursework, bringing new papers and research questions from the institutions and specialties we had entered.

03 / A standing conversation

Once a week,
science has a seat.

The weekly gatherings are not conferences, compulsory seminars or a commercial programme. They are a place where scientists can talk without needing a deliverable before the conversation is worth having. A paper, a synthetic route, a strange result, or a speculative hypothesis can all be reasons to bring everyone together.

Independent thought benefits from company. The questions become sharper when someone else is willing to disagree, suggest an experiment, or expose an assumption that seemed obvious.

01

Bring a question.

Anything from reaction chemistry and natural products to a biomedical or pharmaceutical puzzle.

02

Follow the evidence.

Talk through mechanisms, methods, contradictory findings and the literature behind a claim.

03

Think beyond your own field.

One person's chemistry problem may connect to another's interest in biology, materials or therapeutic design.

04

Keep the conversation alive.

Not every discussion needs to become a publication. Sometimes the reward is a better understanding.

05 / Where the paths led

Separate careers.
A shared beginning.

From the same organic chemistry laboratory, we eventually followed different academic and professional paths. Five of the seven pursued master's-level study: four in Qatar and Ahmed Elgendy in Canada. Our interests expanded into fields including environmental science and solar-cell research, while education became another direction represented in the circle. The original foundation in chemistry remained, but the questions each person brought back grew more varied.

Someone might bring a chemistry problem to the table while another is thinking about biology or engineering. We still meet to exchange papers and challenge interpretations, drawing on the work each of us has pursued.

Some members of the circle are also among the founding members of Majales Education. That connection reflects how a long friendship in science can extend into educational entrepreneurship, while Chem Squad remains its own informal community.

How Chem Squad works

Chem Squad, since 2016.

Chem Squad began as a circle of chemistry students who continued meeting as graduates and researchers. We discuss ideas independently; experiments and papers belong to the researchers and institutions that carry them out. Our early interest in medicinal plants led to questions about composition and biological testing that still require appropriate experimental evidence.

The photographs and accounts here follow the group's early laboratory discussions, friendships and later meetings. Where a member has published research, the individual paper and its authors provide the scientific record.
