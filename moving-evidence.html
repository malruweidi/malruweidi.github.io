Ruwaidix / A moving scientific archive

Moving evidence.

A microscope recording preserves details that disappear in a still photograph. I kept these short films of zebrafish cardiovascular motion, laboratory equipment and scientific conversations because the movements and voices matter to the memory of the work.

01 / Observation of living systems

Under the microscope.

Three short recordings show zebrafish under the microscope. Movement can be observed directly, although its scientific interpretation depends on the imaging conditions and associated measurements.

Film and publication are distinct forms of evidence. The related 2018 ATVB primary-cilia and vascular-development study establishes the published research context; the individual archival clips are not asserted to show any one figure or result from that paper.

02 / Developmental models

An avian preparation.

A separate short recording from chick-embryo work. It belongs to the avian research archive rather than the zebrafish primary-cilia narrative.

03 / Instrumentation

The machinery between results.

Not every recording is a finished experiment. Sometimes its value lies in documenting the physical apparatus and the routine work that remains largely invisible in manuscripts.

04 / Chemistry explained aloud

At the reaction board.

Khaled Jama Mohamoud explains a chemistry reaction associated with his thesis in this original recording. His spoken explanation is preserved with the video.

05 / Institutional record

Research across institutions.

Qatar University Research's recorded seminar discusses international research collaborations through the Qatar University and Medical College of Wisconsin partnership. It is an institutional recording, not original footage owned by this site.

The record stays open.

This index gathers seven already published recordings without changing their original pages. The separate two-minute research documentary connect several of these original records into a longer story. The restored chemistry-reaction recording has now been added with attribution to Khaled Jama Mohamoud; the spoken explanation is preserved without claiming an experimentally validated mechanism. The three historical zebrafish AVI sources are being assessed as archival originals of already featured streams; they are not counted as extra films.
