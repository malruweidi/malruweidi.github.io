RECOMMENDATIONS / ON SCREEN

Science on the screen.

Stories about discovery and its consequences, from the laboratory and the clinic to the history of science and the people whose achievements were overlooked.

Reading note

Dramatic fiction, historical miniseries and biographical films raise important questions without replacing original scientific or historical sources.

ON SCREEN / 08

The screen selection

Thought-provoking cinema and television. Lessons in Chemistry is first overall, and Ikiru is the first film; each entry distinguishes interpretation from documented evidence.

01 / SERIES
Chemistry / scientific independence

Lessons in Chemistry

Apple TV · 2023 · Limited series

Elizabeth Zott’s struggle to pursue chemistry in an institution that dismisses her becomes a story about intellectual independence, discrimination and the art of communicating science to a wider audience.

Explore the official title page ↗
Why it belongs here

Start here. The television adaptation of Bonnie Garmus’s novel complements the book already on the reading shelf.

Dramatic fiction rather than archival laboratory history.
02 / SERIES
Evidence / institutions / nuclear safety

Chernobyl

HBO · 2019 · Historical miniseries

A dramatization of a nuclear disaster in which the concealment of technical evidence and the suppression of dissent become as consequential as the initial failure.

Explore the official title page ↗
Why it belongs here

Read it alongside Feynman’s Cargo Cult Science: the integrity of evidence is an institutional responsibility, not just a personal virtue.

Real historical events, but dialogue, chronology and characters are dramatized.
03 / FILM
Mortality / public service / meaning

Ikiru

Akira Kurosawa · 1952 film

A terminal diagnosis forces an ordinary civil servant to reconsider a life of bureaucratic routines and find meaning through a modest act of public service.

Explore the film ↗
Why it belongs here

The first film on the shelf: an unsentimental study of mortality, agency and the meaning of useful work.

Fictional narrative, discussed in the British Film Institute’s film guide.
04 / FILM
Evidence / prejudice / collective reasoning

12 Angry Men

Sidney Lumet · 1957 film

In a jury room, a single dissenter begins to examine how evidence, stereotypes, confidence and group pressure can lead people toward an irreversible judgment.

Explore the film ↗
Why it belongs here

A reminder that careful reasoning depends on the willingness to reopen assumptions and test what everyone else takes for granted.

A legal drama based on Reginald Rose’s teleplay, not a procedural legal manual.
05 / FILM
Language / uncertainty / time

Arrival

Denis Villeneuve · 2016 film

A linguist works to interpret an unfamiliar language following the appearance of extraterrestrial visitors. The film explores how models of communication shape perception, inference and ideas about time.

Explore the film ↗
Why it belongs here

A compelling fictional thought experiment about translation and the limits of understanding, not a claim that language can rewrite physical time.

Science fiction, not an empirical theory of linguistics or physics.
06 / FILM
Mathematics / recognition / NASA

Hidden Figures

Theodore Melfi · 2016 film

A story about Katherine Johnson, Dorothy Vaughan and Mary Jackson, and the mathematical labor behind the American space program. It examines who gets access to scientific work and who receives credit for doing it.

Explore the official title page ↗
Why it belongs here

A corrective to accounts of scientific history that remember solitary geniuses but overlook collaborative achievement.

Biographical drama with narrative compression.
07 / FILM
Physics / history / scientific ethics

Oppenheimer

Christopher Nolan · 2023 film

The story of a physicist working at the intersection of fundamental discovery, wartime necessity and political authority. A prompt to consider how far scientists remain responsible for technologies that grow out of their research.

Explore the official title page ↗
Why it belongs here

The moral implications of application do not disappear when a discovery leaves the laboratory.

Biographical drama. Historical interpretations should be checked independently.
08 / FILM
Public health / uncertainty / trust

Contagion

Steven Soderbergh · 2011 film

An imagined outbreak told through epidemiological investigation, medical response and rapidly changing public behavior. The film is especially useful for considering how evidence, institutions and public communication interact.

Explore the official title page ↗
Why it belongs here

A way to discuss the translation of scientific inference into public-health decisions under pressure.

Fictional pathogen and compressed timelines; not a documentary about disease response.

These are screen recommendations, not scientific or historical references. For research sources, consult the scholarly bibliography; for the printed inspiration for the opening selection, see Lessons in Chemistry (the novel).
