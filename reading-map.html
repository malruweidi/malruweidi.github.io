RUWAIDIX / AN EDITORIAL READING GUIDE

There is more than one way into the work.

You can begin with an imine sensor, a developing zebrafish vessel, an inhaled drug carrier or a failed histological section. Following any of those questions leads to the experiments, people and practical decisions behind the published record.

Four paths connect the research with its original figures, methods, photographs and first-person accounts. The complete CV, all chapters and the archival collections remain accessible in full.

START HERE / 00

The questions behind the projects.

My projects have moved through chemical synthesis, biomaterials, zebrafish biology, pulmonary epithelial injury and inhalable formulations. Each introduced a new experimental problem: how to identify a product, what a material does at an interface, how flow affects cells, or where an inhaled drug may deposit.

From making a molecule to asking what it can recognize.

Early work with imines, sensors and synthetic chemistry taught me to think in structures and interactions. Later questions pushed beyond the flask: how do molecular properties survive the translation into a useful material or biological environment?

ZEBRAFISH / MECHANICAL INJURY

When a living system refuses to behave like a flask.

Microscopy, developing vasculature and pulmonary epithelial injury brought a different kind of uncertainty. Here, the problem is not simply preparing the material or selecting a measurement. It is deciding what the observation permits you to conclude.

03TRANSPORT

FORMULATION / THE LUNG

A good formulation must survive the journey to its target.

Work on cell injury and my current MSc research on inhalable dexamethasone niosomes and proniosomes share a practical question: what reaches the lung, in what physical state, and with what consequences? Nebulization, aerosol behavior and the response of the biological system matter as much as the initial formulation.
