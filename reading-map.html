START HERE / 00

The question changes when evidence resists.

In synthesis, the planned product may never appear. In microscopy, an image may capture motion without explaining its cause. In delivery research, a promising formulation still has to survive the device and reach a biological target. The common thread is not a technique; it is the discipline of revising a question when the observation no longer supports the expectation.

HOW TO READ

First follow the human and experimental turning points. Then open the original story, methods or paper. A memory explains an experience; a measurement supports a claim. Neither should be asked to do the other's work.

02OBSERVATION

ZEBRAFISH / MECHANICAL INJURY

When observation outpaces explanation.

Microscopy can show movement. An experiment must establish which variables produced it and what consequences follow. Work on developing vasculature and pulmonary epithelial injury brought that distinction into focus: the visible event is not yet the causal account.

03TRANSPORT

FORMULATION / THE LUNG

A good formulation must survive the journey to its target.

Work on cell injury and my current MSc research on inhalable dexamethasone niosomes and proniosomes share a practical question: what reaches the lung, in what physical state, and with what consequences? Nebulization, aerosol behavior and the response of the biological system matter as much as the initial formulation.

THE THREAD CONTINUES

The next question has to earn its answer.

A failed reaction, a living vessel and an unfinished formulation do not tell one seamless success story. They show what changes when a researcher distinguishes the work intended from the result obtained—and the result obtained from the explanation it can support. The original records remain open for anyone who wants to test that distinction.
