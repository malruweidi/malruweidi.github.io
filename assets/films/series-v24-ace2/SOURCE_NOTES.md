## Mechanistic editorial premise

The distinction between **membrane-bound ACE2** (cell-surface ectoenzyme and SARS-CoV-2 entry receptor) and **soluble ACE2** (shed extracellular form) must be explicit and visually legible. Neither form's concentration can be interpreted alone as proof of viral infectivity, disease severity, tissue entry or clinical outcome. ACE2 belongs to the renin–angiotensin system and acts on angiotensin peptides; an extracellular soluble ACE2 pool does not automatically represent cell-surface receptor abundance. ADAM17-related shedding and other regulatory hypotheses must be attributed and qualified to the evidence of this **2021 review**. Avoid treating associations as causal demonstrations.

## Six-chapter cinematic sequence

1. **One protein, different places.** A camera travels from an epithelial membrane into extracellular fluid. Membrane-anchored ACE2 and soluble ACE2 separate in 3D space; molecular forms remain clearly labeled.
2. **A receptor in a larger pathway.** ACE2 participates in the renin–angiotensin network; animated angiotensin peptide conversions and SARS-CoV-2 receptor binding must be represented as *distinct biological roles*. Do not animate an established clinical consequence from the binding alone.
3. **The location changes the question.** A membrane-protein cleavage/shedding concept with movement across membrane and extracellular space. Mark conceptual and avoid assigning unverified kinetic constants or proportions.
4. **Expression is not the whole mechanism.** Animate two simultaneous measurement paths (soluble circulating ACE2 vs tissue membrane ACE2) and a fork into receptor function, enzymatic activity and disease outcomes, with *missing-evidence* visual breaks.
5. **Separate hypothesis from demonstration.** Contrast the 2021 evidence era, assay limitations, tissue specificity, biological plausibility and functional verification. Use original visual abstractions, not a simulated actual patient chart.
6. **Two forms, two questions.** End on separate membrane and soluble forms, with the core editorial questions: **Which form? Which tissue? Which endpoint?**
