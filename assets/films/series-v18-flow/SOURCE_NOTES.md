## Primary research and non-negotiable distinctions

Shurbaji, S.; Al-Ruweidi, M. K. A. A.; Ali, F. H.; Benslimane, F. M.; Yalcin, H. C. *Application of a Flow-Induced Stress Wave and Investigation of Associated Injuries on Cell Monolayers Using a Parallel Plate Flow Chamber.* *Methods and Protocols* **2020**, *3*(4), 65. DOI https://doi.org/10.3390/mps3040065. PubMed PMID **32987681**, PMC **PMC7712398**, publisher open access **CC BY 4.0**. The authors' methodological work is a controlled in-vitro flow-induced airway-reopening model, not a clinical trial and not a numerical simulation of a human bronchus.

The primary article reports a parallel-plate chamber with syringe pumping and coverslips, an air–liquid interface moving over **149 L2** epithelial cells, controls that are PBS-washed and live/dead stained but not stressed, and separate bubble-exposed samples. Experimenters used **calcein** for live cells and **ethidium homodimer-1** for dead-cell fluorescence. A source Figure 5 bar chart reported **around 53%** death in the challenged group and **0–0.2%** death in controls, across **seven repetitions**, with **p < 0.01** from the reported t-test. Never call these exact individual or prospective clinical probabilities; no synthetic confidence interval is introduced.

For cytoskeletal organization, the original paper's Figure 6 shows phalloidin-labelled F-actin (green) and DAPI nuclei (blue) in actual experimental images. After a **single bubble**, the authors did *not* report a statistically significant difference in the quantified actin organization, despite observable orientation differences in representative images. Do not change this null result into proof of a disrupted cytoskeleton.
