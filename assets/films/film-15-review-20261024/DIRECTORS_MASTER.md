# Film 15 — Listening for a Failing Heart

Status: narrative and original-scene development only. No new public release or accepted narrated MP4.

**Source:** H. E. Salman, M. K. A. A. Al-Ruweidi, H. M. Ouakad and H. C. Yalcin, *Minimally Invasive and Non-Invasive Sensor Technologies for Predicting Heart Failure: An Overview*, Wiley, 22 April 2022. https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119813040.ch5

**Running time:** 2:40 / 24 fps / source-first edition.

## Narrative architecture

00:00–00:11 — A heart leaves multiple kinds of traces: potential, motion, light, sound and pressure. No single sensor is definitive.

00:11–00:29 — ECG maps skin-electrode potential differences. Show the actual transduction chain, never invented patient ECG data. Its strength is rhythm and timing, not direct proof of fluid status.

00:29–00:48 — Seismocardiography and ballistocardiography measure different aspects of cardiac-cycle-associated movement. Show an accelerometer axis, contact variation and motion artifacts; label every drawn trace illustrative.

00:48–01:06 — Photoplethysmography: projected light, changing tissue absorption and a photodetector. Separate a proxy physiological signal from direct cardiac output.

01:06–01:23 — Bioimpedance: an applied alternating field, electrodes and an inferred physiological state. Do not assign an invented measured trend or infer an unsourced sign of congestion.

01:23–01:41 — Lung ultrasound and Doppler: acoustic propagation, return signal and flow-related information. Never show fake diagnostic ultrasound footage as experimental or patient evidence.

01:41–01:59 — Implanted pressure monitoring: a different invasiveness and information trade-off from wrist wearables; show actual invasiveness conceptually rather than presenting all techniques as non-invasive.

01:59–02:20 — A measured physical quantity must survive noise, calibration, interpretation and validation before supporting a clinical action. No fictitious patient outcomes or ROC plots.

02:20–02:40 — The 2022 chapter is a literature review, not clinical proof that one modality is superior in every patient. Close on the real chapter title and DOI.
