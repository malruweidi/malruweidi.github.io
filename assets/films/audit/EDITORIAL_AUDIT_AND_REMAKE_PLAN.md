# Ruwaidix — Full Cinema Audit and Director's Remake Plan

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Source:** Actual GitHub repository and six decoded frames from every published documentary MP4.  
**Scope:** **44 finished documentary video files**, not merely the four recent website films; **35** of those are six-scene, 84-second publication and essay adaptations.

## The editorial verdict

The site has a substantial original scientific and photographic archive. The problem is how that archive has been turned into movies. The dominant system assigns six equal scenes to each paper, installs a branded title card and darkened lower third on almost every shot, and fills the image window with a still. The catalog's different style labels do not materially change that grammar. Some scripts make precise scientific distinctions, but the images rarely perform the reasoning for the viewer.

There are exceptions worth protecting. The zebrafish source film uses actual moving microscopic footage; the failed microtomy image is authentic and decisive; the longer air–liquid-interface film contains published numerical findings; the Cairo microscope essay uses genuine archival context and meaningful silence. Those assets must drive the new films, instead of being reduced to small pictures floating over a branded matte.

### What the records prove

- **44 videos decoded and inspected by sampled frames:** nine standalone films plus 35 short research, essay and publication films.
- **17 of 35 shorter films reference three or fewer distinct source images** across their six scenes. This is a catalog count, not a subjective score.
- **Ceramics-printing references one illustration six times.** Fourteen of the fifteen possible pairs among its six decoded sample frames were flagged as visually near-duplicate by a coarse image hash.
- **Curcumin-cyanide repeats its original composite figure across five scenes**, instead of cutting separately between the plotted spectra and the colored-vial evidence.
- **The short medieval pharmacy film repeats one generic apothecary illustration across five scenes.** The longer film is more accurate and differentiated, but still relies mainly on flat explanatory artwork rather than compelling material or historical evidence.
- **A sampled frame of the 180-second ring-closure documentary is entirely black.** Continuous playback inspection is needed to determine whether this is a deliberate transition or an unintended dead frame; the sample alone cannot establish duration.
- A majority of the shorts use precisely the same 84-second six-shot syntax. A film's topic is not an editing decision.

The film catalog, decoded contact sheets, and source-variation records are in [full-film-inventory.json](./full-film-inventory.json) and the [contact sheet gallery](./contact-sheets/). Numbers here describe documented assets and frames. **They do not constitute a human listening review or an audience study.**

## A stronger cinematic rulebook

**Start with the material, not the brand.** A machine touching paraffin, a bubble advancing across cells, crystals forming in syrup, and a colleague explaining a reaction are more useful openings than a logo or abstract title. Where there is no authentic footage, introduce an expressly labeled reconstruction, or let a real document speak.

**One film, one developed tension.** A research question alone is not a plot. Show what was expected, what was measured or resisted, and how the result changed the next question. A review article has a different drama: competing models and boundaries of interpretation. An essay can have an argument as its turn rather than a laboratory event.

**Source-first editing.** Locate, license, and verify the best scientific figure, research photograph, manuscript or moving evidence before locking narration. The current catalog usually starts with a script and then assigns whatever images exist. Reverse that order.

**No fixed scene count or mandatory voice.** Short works may be 50 seconds or three minutes, depending on evidence. Give a face time to register, show a figure with its axes, or allow an experiment's outcome to be silent. Not every manuscript needs the same synthetic narrator and the same harmonic bed. For deeply personal essays, use the author's approved first-person voice, restrained onscreen text, or narration explicitly approved by him.

**Scientific graphics must be readable.** First show the full original figure with its provenance, then move into an intentional magnified region or redraw a chart *from its source values*, not from guessed traces. Leave room for captions without shrinking the critical data area.

**Use archives truthfully.** Contextual lab photographs are not a record of a particular experiment. A photo with a colleague is not video of the Chem Squad meeting. The Genizah folio cannot be reproduced without necessary permission. No identifiable voice clones, fake patient records, invented spectra, or fabricated outcomes.

**Sound tells the truth too.** Original microphone audio from licensed footage should be used when its context is verified. Sound design can be illustrative, but do not pass synthetic Foley as the sound of a historic experiment. Keep spoken language clear and human. LUFS measurements and correctly timed captions cannot substitute for an editorial audition.

**One preferred release per story.** There are multiple versions of histology failure, Cairo microscope, pulmonary injury and historical pharmacy. Select one strongest director's cut for primary navigation. Preserve earlier cuts and their links as an annotated version history, as the website's preservation rule requires.

## Three pilot director's cuts before a 44-film batch

### Pilot A — *Two Materials. No Section.*
The first image should be the actual failed microtome attempt. No title for several seconds; the material occupies the frame. A second shot returns to the small specimens: now we understand why their orientation mattered. The evidence moves forward through the real embedding photographs, and one brief, explicit hypothesis drawing explains why agarose and paraffin may have separated. The narrative finishes with the absence of a usable ribbon. Avoid heroic music and conclusive diagnosis of the failure mechanism. **The incomplete result is the climax.**

### Pilot B — *Making Medicines Keep*
A material history, not a medieval mood reel. Open on syrup behavior in an **explicitly modern illustrative preparation**, filmed or commissioned with provenance. Let the audience see a difference between a stable pour and crystals in a vessel. The sources then introduce *julāb* and *qiwām*, framed as historical judgments of preparation. A real licensed manuscript page should only be shown with the correct attribution and permissions; otherwise use author-created type and an external catalogue link. Substitution becomes the film's dilemma, and the accepted article arrives at the end, not as opening self-promotion.

### Pilot C — *The Laboratory We Carried With Us*
Faces need time and size. Use verified group photographs full-width or with intentional reframing rather than leaving most of the frame as a blur. Differentiate QUACS and Chem Squad using original dated archive captions. Short excerpts from *authentic* colleague chemistry footage may appear as later testimony only when their original context and permission are established. Retain real room sounds when available. This is a film about how a group of young chemists continued to make time for questions, not a montage of awards.

Each pilot receives a **script, shot-by-shot archive ledger, 20–30-second sample edit, full director's cut, two-screen mobile check, and independent scientific review** before replacing a featured film.

## Quality and release gates

A revised film cannot be designated 'completed' because an encoder returned exit code zero. Every release must pass:

1. **Evidence:** exact source IDs, rights/permissions for third-party material, original versus reconstruction visibly identified, no fictitious data, no credit inflation.
2. **Narrative:** a distinguishable opening, conflict/question, evidentiary turn, and convincing end. No identical sequence of generic labels across an entire collection.
3. **Picture:** actual representative-frame review from the exported MP4; important material readable at phone size; no accidental black frames, letterboxed postage-stamp subjects or six shots of one undifferentiated graphic.
4. **Sound:** human listening, pronunciation review and music/voice balance, plus measurable peaks and loudness.
5. **Accessibility and preservation:** checked captions, meaningful transcript, Arabic translation after wording is verified, responsive playback; preserve prior film versions and scientific files.

## No premature deployment

This is a **non-destructive preproduction audit**. Existing website films have not been replaced by the audit. The decisive next step is one independently directed proof-of-quality, not automatically re-rendering 44 differently titled slide decks.
