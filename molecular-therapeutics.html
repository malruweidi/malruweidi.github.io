CHEMISTRY / LIFE SCIENCES / PHARMACEUTICAL & BIOMEDICAL ENGINEERING

Molecules, materials and living systems.

I came to pharmaceutical sciences through chemistry, then learned to work with materials, imaging and living systems. My research experience ranges from chemical sensing and polymer interfaces to vascular development, mechanically injured lung epithelium, computational imaging and inhalable drug carriers. I remain interested in fundamental experimental questions as well as their eventual application to medicine.

I am open to doctoral research in chemistry, chemical biology, biochemistry, molecular and experimental pharmacology, pharmaceutics, biomaterials, bioengineering, and related biomedical sciences. I do not require a project to involve inhalation, RNA, protein degradation, a particular disease or one of the researchers discussed below. What matters is a scientifically substantial problem, rigorous methods and the opportunity to develop real experimental depth.

03 / ONGOING

What I am working on now.

My MSc research at Qatar University concerns dexamethasone-loaded niosomes and proniosomes intended for pulmonary delivery. The investigation involves formulation design, physicochemical characterization, nebulization and aerosol-performance questions. A stable carrier in a vial is not necessarily a useful carrier after administration; aerosol behavior, tissue exposure and biological activity must be established separately.

This project is ongoing. I do not present proposed or incomplete outcomes as demonstrated efficacy. My academic history also includes imine chemical sensing, polymeric and electrospun materials, cell-based mechanobiology, imaging and quantitative scientific workflows.

Molecular therapeutics: questions that interest me.

MOLECULAR RECOGNITION / IMMUNOPHARMACOLOGY

Changing how cells communicate.

Drugs that interfere with protein–protein interactions can change how receptors communicate, but binding alone says little about pathway function. In the work of Moustafa T. Gabr and collaborators, CD28 antagonists inhibit T-cell costimulation, while TREM2 agonists are evaluated through downstream microglial signalling. His group has also identified ROBO2-binding chemical starting points, a direct scientific connection to my own SLIT2–ROBO patent-review interests.

My question: can chemical probes distinguish receptor occupancy from antagonism, agonism and a meaningful cell-state change? I'd connect biophysical assays, cellular engagement, downstream signalling and a phenotype, not just docking scores.

A pharmacological lesson from a different organ: Rasha Alshaikh's work with Katie Ryan and Christian Waeber on siponimod links S1P-receptor biology to endothelial migration, vascular barrier function, in vivo angiogenesis, ocular pharmacokinetics and sustained PLGA delivery. The disease context is retinal, not pulmonary; what transfers is the disciplined chain from receptor action through tissue exposure to functional endpoints. EPIGENETICS / QUANTITATIVE PHARMACOLOGY

What did the drug change inside the cell?

Ted Lakowski's pharmaceutical analysis research at the University of Manitoba is a model of an approach I admire: pairing epigenetic enzyme pharmacology with LC–MS/MS measurements of histone and DNA modifications, drugs and metabolites. HDAC and DOT1L inhibition can alter chromatin chemistry, but a transcriptomic shift should not automatically be read as specific target engagement.

My question: can exposure, enzyme activity, histone-mark changes, downstream gene expression and a cellular phenotype be measured as one causal chain? This would require developing analytical and pharmacological expertise, not simply running an RNA-seq experiment.

PROGRAMMABLE RNA / THERAPEUTIC FUNCTION

Silence a harmful signal, or restore a useful one?

siRNA recruits RNA interference to reduce a transcript; protein-coding circular RNA instead offers a route to sustained expression, provided its translation elements, circularity, purity and delivery work. These are not interchangeable medicines. I am particularly interested in engineering circular-RNA cargo and ionizable lipid carriers while using siRNA as a mechanistically different, hypothesis-dependent comparator, not simply copying an existing pulmonary-siRNA project.

My question: when is gene suppression preferable to functional replacement? In pulmonary vascular biology, BMPR2–SMAD1/5/9–ID1 signalling offers a concrete restorative example: BMPR2 linear mRNA delivery improved experimental rat PAH outcomes; a circular-RNA alternative remains a proposed extension and must be tested separately.

A pulmonary RNA question worth separating: work associated with Katie B. Ryan at UCC tests what happens to siRNA lipoplexes and LNPs during vibrating-mesh nebulisation, from aerosol formation through retained functional silencing. That problem is directly relevant to my experience with inhalable niosomes, but siRNA knockdown and circular-RNA protein expression require different measures of therapeutic success. Neary et al., Eur. J. Pharm. Sci. (2026) ↗ (published corrigendum ↗).

CHEMICAL ENGINEERING / MODELS / PK–PD

A dose delivered is not a dose that acts.

Microfluidics interests me in two distinct ways: as controlled-mixing technology for reproducible lipid or polymeric carriers, and as a way to create perfused tissue environments where flow, barrier integrity and disease state alter exposure. This connects directly to my work on mechanically injured lung epithelium, and to my current dexamethasone niosome/proniosome thesis. It also opens up systems beyond the lung, including tumour, immune and vascular tissues.

My question: which barrier prevents productive action: carrier destabilization, extracellular transport, cellular uptake, endosomal escape, intracellular pharmacology or tissue-level compensation? An organ-on-chip is useful only if its predicted response can be validated against simpler models and appropriate controls.

Recognition must be measurable: Jing Yong Ye's UTSA group has reported modified-aptamer functionalization of photonic-crystal biosensors, and co-developed a liver-on-chip platform for label-free monitoring of drug-induced toxicity. For me, the transferable engineering questions are binding specificity, aptamer conformation and stability, signal calibration, and how an affinity readout differs from productive drug delivery. Modified aptamers for biosensing (SPIE proceedings) ↗ · Liver-on-chip study ↗.

Microfluidic lung models ↗ · My lung-cell study ↗ · Current thesis ↗

One more chemical thread: I want to develop the skills to construct bioorthogonal conjugates, clickable ligands and bifunctional degraders. PROTACs and molecular glues are interesting when the target biology demands protein removal rather than occupancy, but solubility, intracellular exposure, target resynthesis and off-target degradation must be measured. Click chemistry is a synthetic method, not a pharmacological mechanism. Read the chemical-biology review ↗.

06 / EXAMPLE STUDIES

How questions become testable experiments.

The next two concepts are worked examples of designing a hypothesis and testing it. They demonstrate how I think, not the only projects I would accept. In a chemistry, biomaterials, biology, pharmaceutics, computational or engineering laboratory, I would develop a project around the laboratory’s expertise and a question that can be answered with available methods. PROTAC synthesis, circular-RNA engineering and organ-on-chip construction remain future training goals, not skills I already claim to possess.

Target engagement through chemical design.

Choose one disease-relevant protein state and test whether a carefully designed intervention changes its function. I want to learn and use bioorthogonal click chemistry and bioconjugation, bifunctional degraders such as PROTACs, molecular glues and aptamer-based recognition where the biology justifies each tool. Pair molecular characterization with target engagement, degradation or functional rescue, rather than relying on binding alone.

Testable chemical-biology concept ↗ · Research horizon ↗

Delivery as a pharmacological experiment.

Choose a justified therapeutic cargo, distinguishing siRNA-mediated silencing from protein-producing circular RNA, alongside aptamer conjugates or targeted small molecules, and determine where productive exposure is lost. Develop lipid/polymeric or other appropriate carriers, investigate microfluidic formulation and transport, and measure intracellular fate and functional dose response in relevant cellular models. Connect formulation engineering to PK/PD and mechanistic predictions.

A microfluidic organ-on-chip or organoid model could then test a specific tissue barrier or disease state, but its predictive validity must be measured, not presumed. I want to connect molecular chemistry, engineering and quantitative pharmacology through experiments that could ultimately support translation from bench to bedside.

07 / SCIENTIFIC READING

Ideas I learn from across fields.

These selected publications illustrate scientific approaches I admire, not a list of laboratories I will apply to, required future supervisors or research programmes I insist on copying. I read across disciplines because their methods illuminate the problems in my own experimental work.

Moustafa T. Gabr connects medicinal chemistry, target discovery and functional validation in difficult receptor systems, including CD28, TREM2 and the SLIT–ROBO family. The ROBO2 connection is particularly close to my prior SLIT2/ROBO literature work, while the translational imaging dimension suggests a wider future direction. Columbia research profile ↗.

Ted Lakowski brings pharmacology back to measurement: epigenetic enzyme activity, histone and DNA marks, drug/metabolite analysis and the distinction between mechanistic evidence and a broad genomic response. University of Manitoba profile ↗.

Katie B. Ryan (University College Cork) works across formulation science and RNA delivery, including the experimentally difficult transition from a nebulised siRNA formulation to cytosolic activity. The 2024 aerosol-to-cytosol review and 2026 vibrating-mesh nebulisation study are especially close to the questions I now face with inhalable niosomes: what is the delivered dose, what survives processing, and what constitutes a functional intracellular response? RNA aerosol barriers ↗ · siRNA nanocarrier nebulisation ↗.

Rasha A. Alshaikh (University College Cork) approaches therapeutic performance through pharmacology and controlled release rather than RNA aerosolisation: her published work investigates siponimod modulation of S1P signalling, retinal endothelial cell behaviour, rabbit ocular pharmacokinetics and sustained release from electrospun PLGA. The conceptual connection to my research is how a molecular mechanism, controlled exposure and tissue physiology constrain one another. It is not a claim that retinal neovascularization results establish a pulmonary RNA target. Mechanism and angiogenesis ↗ · Ocular PK and toxicity ↗ · Controlled delivery ↗.

Jing Yong Ye (University of Texas at San Antonio) is a distinct bridge between my optical chemical-sensing and cardiovascular-imaging background and future aptamer research. His group has investigated aptamer-functionalized photonic-crystal biosensors for cardiac biomarkers, activatable photoacoustic sensing, targeted nanocarriers and organ-on-chip label-free readouts. An aptamer can be a recognition element for a sensor without yet being a therapeutically useful targeting ligand. I am interested in learning how structural modelling, affinity and specificity measurements, nuclease and serum stability, and functional validation change the choice of aptamer or conjugate. Ye laboratory research ↗ · Aptamer photonic-crystal biosensor ↗ · Integrated liver-on-chip biosensing ↗.

Robert Chen and colleagues provide a rigorous example of optimizing protein-producing circular RNA by dissecting IRES, translation-initiation and RNA-structure determinants rather than simply assuming circularity is a therapeutic advantage. Nature Biotechnology study ↗.

What attracts me across those programmes is the same discipline: design one intervention, establish what it does molecularly, and test whether that change persists at the level of biological function.

08 / RESEARCH DEVELOPMENT

A researcher in development, not a fixed specialty.

What I bring now: documented experimental chemistry and materials research, cell-based physiological experiments, microscopy and imaging-system implementation, formulation development, quantitative interpretation, scientific writing and collaborative computational research. Each project has its own author-contribution boundaries; the record and source papers document those differences.

Where I could grow: organic and medicinal synthesis; chemical biology and advanced analytical methods; biochemistry and molecular pharmacology; drug formulation, transport and PK/PD; polymer and biomaterial engineering; cell and tissue models; microfluidic platforms; and computational or imaging methods. Specialized interests such as aptamers, siRNA, circular RNA, PROTACs and targeted conjugates may be relevant when a project calls for them. I do not expect one PhD to teach or deploy all these technologies.

I value careful experimental reasoning regardless of whether the immediate output is a new molecule, an instrument, a biomaterial, a biological mechanism, a dosage form or a therapeutic candidate. For translational projects, I am particularly interested in connecting chemical design to exposure and functional response. For fundamental projects, a strong mechanistic result is worthwhile in its own right.
