Essays worth revisiting.

From Feynman and Medawar on research integrity to Woolf and Gould on illness, these twelve essays explore how knowledge is produced, argued and lived.

THE ESSAYS / 12

The essays

Twelve arguments spanning basic research, honest reporting, information systems, literature, medicine, attention and the experience of illness.

01 / ESSAY
Scientific integrity / essay

Cargo Cult Science

Richard P. Feynman · 1974

Feynman’s Caltech commencement address is a compact lesson in the difference between the appearance of research and the discipline of honest inquiry. The decisive test is whether an investigator discloses the evidence, uncertainties and experimental defects that might weaken their preferred explanation.

Read at Caltech ↗Original publication record ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

For any scientific project, ask what result would change your mind and whether you have made it possible for that result to appear.

First published in Engineering and Science, 37(7), pp. 10–13.
02 / ESSAY
Scientific writing / epistemology · Featured

Is the Scientific Paper a Fraud?

Peter Medawar · 1963

Medawar questions the convention by which research papers make scientific inquiry appear far more orderly than discovery actually is. Hypotheses, false starts and imagination tend to disappear from the final narrative.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

A candid account of research need not pretend that investigation follows a prewritten script.

Oxford Academic chapter; full access may require a subscription.
03 / ESSAY
Basic research / intellectual freedom · Featured

The Usefulness of Useless Knowledge

Abraham Flexner · 1939

A spirited defense of basic research pursued without immediate application. Flexner shows why intellectual freedom and curiosity can lead, sometimes indirectly, to enormous practical benefit.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

A researcher cannot know in advance which apparently remote question will matter most.

Original essay published in Harper’s Magazine, 1939.
04 / ESSAY
Knowledge systems / scientific computing

As We May Think

Vannevar Bush · 1945

Bush imagines methods of information retrieval that let people create associative trails through records and ideas, anticipating important parts of the intellectual architecture of hypertext.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Scientific tools are not merely instruments for answering questions; they change which questions become possible.

An influential historical vision, not a modern technical specification.
05 / ESSAY
Literary craft / intellectual independence

Why I Write

George Orwell · 1946

Orwell examines ambition, aesthetic enthusiasm and political purpose as motives for authorship. The result is an unusually unsentimental investigation of a writer’s own incentives.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Especially worthwhile when moving between scholarly writing and personal essays.

An author’s self-account rather than a universal theory of creativity.
06 / ESSAY
Attention / moral imagination

This Is Water

David Foster Wallace · 2005

A commencement address about habitual interpretations, other people and the discipline of conscious attention. It asks a question deeper than how to concentrate: what deserves concentration?

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Read it alongside Deep Work, while distinguishing focused productivity from ethical attentiveness.

The original 2005 commencement speech, preserved by Kenyon College.
07 / ESSAY
Anthropology / narrative theory

The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction

Ursula K. Le Guin · 1986

Le Guin challenges heroic stories of conquest and imagines the bag or vessel, a technology of gathering and sustaining, as an alternative organizing metaphor for human history and fiction.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

A welcome corrective to versions of scientific history organized entirely around individual heroes.

The author’s website discusses the essay and later published editions.
08 / ESSAY
Medicine / clinical quality

The Bell Curve

Atul Gawande · 2004

Gawande examines variation in medical outcomes and the professional obligations that follow from recognizing uneven performance. Statistical comparison becomes an issue of institutional ethics.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

An elegant bridge between clinical measurement, professional responsibility and system improvement.

Reported medical narrative; read findings in their original clinical context.
09 / ESSAY
Neurology / perception

To See and Not See

Oliver Sacks · 1993

A patient regains sight but confronts the difference between sensory function and meaningful visual perception. Sacks approaches physiology with sustained attention to the patient’s identity.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Treat the person and the biological mechanism as related but not interchangeable subjects.

Literary case history; a single patient narrative cannot establish general causality.
10 / ESSAY
Statistics / illness / prognosis

The Median Isn't the Message

Stephen Jay Gould · 1991; reprinted 2013

Gould reflects on his cancer diagnosis to explain why a population median cannot prescribe any individual’s prognosis. The essay makes statistical variation humane without abandoning quantitative reasoning.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

An excellent example of why clinicians must explain distributions rather than turn summary statistics into predictions of destiny.

2013 AMA Journal of Ethics reprint from Gould’s 1991 essay collection.
11 / ESSAY
Illness / literary imagination

On Being Ill

Virginia Woolf · 1926

Woolf asks why illness, which can transform consciousness and the perception of time, has had so little prominence in literature. Her prose insists that the experience of bodily vulnerability deserves artistic seriousness.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

An important companion to writing about illness without reducing it to a lesson in perseverance.

Original 1926 Criterion text; later versions were revised.
12 / ESSAY
Scientific celebrity / cultural criticism

The Cult of Richard Feynman

J. Michael Kennedy · 2001

Kennedy examines the legend surrounding Feynman and the transformation of a serious scientist into a cultural personality. It complements Cargo Cult Science by turning scrutiny toward the stories told about scientists themselves.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Admire Feynman’s intellectual discipline without mistaking the mythology of genius for scientific method.

2001 Los Angeles Times feature; journalism rather than an archival biography.
