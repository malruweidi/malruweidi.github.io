Twelve arguments spanning basic research, honest reporting, information systems, literature, medicine, attention and the experience of illness.

01 / ESSAY
Scientific integrity / essay

Cargo Cult Science

Richard P. Feynman · 1974

Feynman’s Caltech commencement address is a compact lesson in the difference between the appearance of research and the discipline of honest inquiry. The decisive test is whether an investigator discloses the evidence, uncertainties and experimental defects that might weaken their preferred explanation.

Read at Caltech ↗Original publication record ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

For any scientific project, ask what result would change your mind and whether you have made it possible for that result to appear.

First published in Engineering and Science, 37(7), pp. 10–13.
02 / ESSAY
Scientific writing / epistemology · Featured

Is the Scientific Paper a Fraud?

Peter Medawar · 1963

Medawar questions the convention by which research papers make scientific inquiry appear far more orderly than discovery actually is. Hypotheses, false starts and imagination tend to disappear from the final narrative.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

A candid account of research need not pretend that investigation follows a prewritten script.

Oxford Academic chapter; full access may require a subscription.
03 / ESSAY
Basic research / intellectual freedom · Featured

The Usefulness of Useless Knowledge

Abraham Flexner · 1939

A spirited defense of basic research pursued without immediate application. Flexner shows why intellectual freedom and curiosity can lead, sometimes indirectly, to enormous practical benefit.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

A researcher cannot know in advance which apparently remote question will matter most.

Original essay published in Harper’s Magazine, 1939.
04 / ESSAY
Knowledge systems / scientific computing

As We May Think

Vannevar Bush · 1945

Bush imagines methods of information retrieval that let people create associative trails through records and ideas, anticipating important parts of the intellectual architecture of hypertext.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Scientific tools are not merely instruments for answering questions; they change which questions become possible.

An influential historical vision, not a modern technical specification.
05 / ESSAY
Literary craft / intellectual independence

Why I Write

George Orwell · 1946

Orwell examines ambition, aesthetic enthusiasm and political purpose as motives for authorship. The result is an unusually unsentimental investigation of a writer’s own incentives.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Especially worthwhile when moving between scholarly writing and personal essays.

An author’s self-account rather than a universal theory of creativity.
06 / ESSAY
Attention / moral imagination

This Is Water

David Foster Wallace · 2005

A commencement address about habitual interpretations, other people and the discipline of conscious attention. It asks a question deeper than how to concentrate: what deserves concentration?

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Read it alongside Deep Work, while distinguishing focused productivity from ethical attentiveness.

The original 2005 commencement speech, preserved by Kenyon College.
07 / ESSAY
Anthropology / narrative theory

The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction

Ursula K. Le Guin · 1986

Le Guin challenges heroic stories of conquest and imagines the bag or vessel, a technology of gathering and sustaining, as an alternative organizing metaphor for human history and fiction.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

A welcome corrective to versions of scientific history organized entirely around individual heroes.

The author’s website discusses the essay and later published editions.
08 / ESSAY
Medicine / clinical quality

The Bell Curve

Atul Gawande · 2004

Gawande examines variation in medical outcomes and the professional obligations that follow from recognizing uneven performance. Statistical comparison becomes an issue of institutional ethics.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

An elegant bridge between clinical measurement, professional responsibility and system improvement.

Reported medical narrative; read findings in their original clinical context.
09 / ESSAY
Neurology / perception

To See and Not See

Oliver Sacks · 1993

A patient regains sight but confronts the difference between sensory function and meaningful visual perception. Sacks approaches physiology with sustained attention to the patient’s identity.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Treat the person and the biological mechanism as related but not interchangeable subjects.

Literary case history; a single patient narrative cannot establish general causality.
10 / ESSAY
Statistics / illness / prognosis

The Median Isn't the Message

Stephen Jay Gould · 1991; reprinted 2013

Gould reflects on his cancer diagnosis to explain why a population median cannot prescribe any individual’s prognosis. The essay makes statistical variation humane without abandoning quantitative reasoning.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

An excellent example of why clinicians must explain distributions rather than turn summary statistics into predictions of destiny.

2013 AMA Journal of Ethics reprint from Gould’s 1991 essay collection.
11 / ESSAY
Illness / literary imagination

On Being Ill

Virginia Woolf · 1926

Woolf asks why illness, which can transform consciousness and the perception of time, has had so little prominence in literature. Her prose insists that the experience of bodily vulnerability deserves artistic seriousness.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

An important companion to writing about illness without reducing it to a lesson in perseverance.

Original 1926 Criterion text; later versions were revised.
12 / ESSAY
Scientific celebrity / cultural criticism

The Cult of Richard Feynman

J. Michael Kennedy · 2001

Kennedy examines the legend surrounding Feynman and the transformation of a serious scientist into a cultural personality. It complements Cargo Cult Science by turning scrutiny toward the stories told about scientists themselves.

Open the source ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Admire Feynman’s intellectual discipline without mistaking the mythology of genius for scientific method.

2001 Los Angeles Times feature; journalism rather than an archival biography.
13 / ESSAY
Discovery / disciplinary boundaries · FEATURED

Why Science Needs Outsiders

Alvin Djajadikerta & Laura Lungu · 2025

A case for the contributions of researchers whose training, employment or perspectives fall outside established specialties. The authors argue that specialization and institutional barriers can leave fewer paths for unconventional inquiry.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Worth reading as an argument for intellectual permeability, while scrutinizing the historical examples and what counts as an outsider.

Works in Progress, 18 September 2025. An argumentative essay, not a systematic historical survey.
14 / ESSAY
Personal websites / creative independence · FEATURED

My Website Is a Shifting House Next to a River of Knowledge. What Could Yours Be?

Laurel Schwulst · 2018

A poetic case for treating the personal website as a changing, self-authored space rather than a fixed professional portfolio. The website becomes a place where accumulated knowledge changes the architecture itself.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

A particularly fitting text for an evolving research-and-writing website that should retain the freedom to change as its creator does.

The Creative Independent, 21 May 2018.
15 / ESSAY
Original work / research direction

How to Do Great Work

Paul Graham · 2023

An extended essay on choosing important problems, developing taste, reaching the frontier of a discipline and noticing anomalies others overlook. It emphasizes sustained interest as a means of identifying promising work.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

A useful set of heuristics for intellectual careers, best treated as one practitioner’s theory rather than empirical instruction.

Paul Graham’s website, July 2023.
16 / ESSAY
Illness / medical humanities

Being Ill, Living Well

Ian J. Kidd · 2016

Kidd considers whether illness can change a person’s understanding of the good life, without making virtue or gratitude a requirement of suffering. He draws on philosophy of illness and the distinction between clinical disease and lived experience.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Illness need not be redeemed by a moral lesson for the patient’s account to command respect.

Aeon, 8 November 2016.
17 / ESSAY
Research policy / technology

Innovation Is Not Linear

Jason Crawford · 2020

A challenge to the notion that technological progress advances in a one-way sequence from basic science through applied research to products. Invention and scientific understanding can also develop reciprocally.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Especially useful for thinking about how molecular discovery and therapeutic engineering can shape one another.

Works in Progress, 19 October 2020.
18 / ESSAY
Philosophy / personal identity

Essence Is Fluttering

Alexander Douglas · 2025

Douglas draws on Zhuangzi’s butterfly dream to question whether a person has a fixed essence. The essay explores transformation and the possibility of living without trying to enforce a single immutable self.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

A philosophical companion to reflections on becoming a different person over time.

Aeon, 1 September 2025.
19 / ESSAY
Attention / creative practice

On How to Grow an Idea

Jenny Odell · 2018

An invitation to cultivate observation and patience instead of treating creativity as a predictable production process. The piece uses metaphors of growth and emergence to describe intellectual work.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Not every fruitful day produces a measurable output; sometimes time spent looking is part of the work.

The Creative Independent, 4 April 2018.
20 / ESSAY
Replication / scientific authority

How Trust Undermines Science

Sarah Perry · 2021

A polemical examination of misplaced deference to scientific claims, the replication crisis and the social barriers that can discourage challenge. It argues that proper respect for science includes evaluating its evidence critically.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Read as a provocative position to test against methodology and data, not an indictment of the entire scientific enterprise.

Works in Progress, 14 September 2021.
21 / ESSAY
History of science / interdisciplinary inquiry · FEATURED

The Missing Conversation

Lorraine Daston & Peter Harrison · 2024

Daston and Harrison argue that historians and scientists need to recover a productive dialogue about authorship, institutional values, the origins of methods and changing claims to authority. Historical understanding becomes a practical resource for contemporary science.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

One of the strongest selections here: history can illuminate assumptions that working scientists have stopped noticing.

Aeon, 5 January 2024. Also published under the article’s descriptive URL title.
22 / ESSAY
Evidence synthesis / research quality · FEATURED

How to Assemble Science

Helen Pearson · 2026

Pearson examines why enormous volumes of individual studies do not automatically accumulate into reliable knowledge. Evidence synthesis, transparent review methods and careful communication are essential to understanding what research has already established.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

A reminder that making science useful often requires rigorous integration of existing findings rather than another isolated paper.

Aeon, 6 October 2026. Original heading: How to assemble science.
23 / ESSAY
Literary nonfiction / relationships

The Crane Wife

CJ Hauser · 2019

A personal essay combining a broken engagement, a field expedition and a Japanese folktale. The narrative uses the natural world and folklore to explore what people surrender when they reshape themselves to remain loved.

Read the essay ↗

An example of personal writing that earns its emotional force through observation and structure rather than easy sentiment.

The Paris Review, 16 July 2019.

Diagnostic uncertainty / medical humanities · FEATURED

Permission to Be Ill

Kevin Aho · 2025

Aho recounts his experience of functional neurological disorder and the difficulties of obtaining a credible diagnosis and coordinated care. The essay explores how a disease can be disabling even when conventional tests do not identify structural pathology.

Read the essay ↗

A powerful starting point for discussing epistemic humility, patient testimony and the clinical consequences of uncertainty.

Aeon, 16 May 2025. Original heading: Permission to be ill.
25 / ESSAY
Curiosity / research originality

The Bus Ticket Theory of Genius

Paul Graham · 2019

Graham suggests that unusual, persistent curiosity about subjects others dismiss can generate distinctive discoveries. His essay treats obsession with a question as a potential ingredient of original work.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

An intriguing hypothesis about creativity, but the mechanisms and examples do not establish a scientific theory of genius.

Paul Graham’s website, 2019.
26 / ESSAY
Time / choosing priorities

Life Is Short

Paul Graham · 2016

A reflection on how apparently abundant time becomes scarce when counted as particular experiences, relationships and opportunities. It argues for paying more attention to what deserves one’s limited days.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Worth placing beside The Tail End, while avoiding the temptation to reduce the value of a life to maximized productivity.

Paul Graham’s website, January 2016.
27 / ESSAY
Life / time with others

The Tail End

Tim Urban · 2015

Urban depicts human life through the number of remaining shared experiences rather than calendar years. His visual accounting of time with family and friends exposes how relationships can enter a final chapter long before anyone realizes it.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

An accessible prompt to protect time with people who matter, not a statistical prediction of anyone’s lifespan.

Wait But Why, 11 December 2015.
28 / ESSAY
Literature / illness narratives

Why Is Illness So Hard to Write About?

Hermione Hoby · 2026

A contemporary reconsideration of Woolf’s On Being Ill and the difficulties literature faces in representing bodily suffering. Hoby examines the tension between vivid personal experience and the forms usually available for telling it.

Read the essay ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Read beside Woolf’s original: a century later, illness still challenges the language and conventions of literary narrative.

The New Yorker, online October 2026; issue dated 12 October 2026.
29 / ESSAY
Medicine / uncertainty / professional judgment · FEATURED

Learning to Not-Know

Zoe Cunniffe · 2025 · Also titled “Medicine Is Uncertain: Embracing That Makes Doctors Better”

A thoughtful examination of uncertainty in clinical practice: why physicians may hesitate to admit what they do not know, how that silence can undermine trust, and how transparent communication and continued diagnostic inquiry can improve care. Cunniffe considers both medical decision-making and the patient’s experience of being difficult to diagnose.

Read at Aeon ↗
Why it stays on the shelf

Admitting uncertainty need not mean abandoning clinical rigor. A better response is to make assumptions explicit, communicate risk and establish a plan for what happens next.

Aeon, 2025. “Learning to not-know” is the on-page heading; the URL and search title use “Medicine is uncertain.”
