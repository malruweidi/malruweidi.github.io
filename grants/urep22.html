QNRF undergraduate research / UREP22-076-1-011

Development of medical scaffolds with enhanced infection resistance using electrospinning/plasma technology

Fabrication and surface modification of PLA scaffolds for tissue engineering and antimicrobial properties.

22nd UREP cycle · research plan dated 2017 · Research team at Qatar University · Undergraduate researcher contribution

Scientific question

Biomaterials engineering and tissue–material interfaces

The project addressed a common problem in tissue-engineering scaffolds: otherwise useful polymer substrates may offer poor resistance to microbial adhesion. Its strategy combined electrospinning with plasma-assisted surface modification of polylactic acid.

This work contributed to a continuing biomaterials research trajectory from 2018 onward, alongside later work in nanofibrous surfaces and biomedical textiles. Related coauthored peer-reviewed publications document plasma-modified PLA materials, but this page does not assume that every proposed scaffold property was proven within the original award period.

PROJECT SCOPE / ATTRIBUTION

Student research role within a supervised Qatar University team. The original QNRF research plan provides the project scope, with subsequent outputs identified separately.

Methods and research design

  • Electrospinning of polylactic-acid nanofiber mats
  • Low-temperature plasma surface activation
  • Grafting of ascorbic and fumaric acid functionality
  • Surface wettability, morphology and antimicrobial-characterization concepts

Research record

Related paper: Popelka et al., Surface and Coatings Technology (2020), 400, 126216; related PLA surface chemistry: Polymers (2021), 13, 3717.
