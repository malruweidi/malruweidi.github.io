QNRF undergraduate research / UREP19-110-1-014

Polymeric micelles for remediation of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons

Self-assembled block-copolymer micelles for solubilizing and recovering hydrophobic environmental pollutants.

19th UREP cycle · research plan dated 2016 · Research team at Qatar University · Undergraduate researcher contribution

Scientific question

Polymer physical chemistry and colloidal transport

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons have limited aqueous solubility. The project investigated whether the hydrophobic cores of block-copolymer micelles could increase apparent solubility and enable improved remediation. It connected polymer thermodynamics to a practical separation problem.

My student-research experience included examining the physicochemical basis of micellar solubilization and research formulation questions. The archived plan establishes the project scope, but should not be treated as proof that every proposed experiment or performance target was completed.

PROJECT SCOPE / ATTRIBUTION

Student research role within a supervised Qatar University team. The original QNRF research plan provides the project scope, with subsequent outputs identified separately.

Methods and research design

  • Amphiphilic block-copolymer self-assembly and micellization
  • Critical micelle concentration, aggregation and hydrophobic-core partitioning
  • Thermodynamic reasoning and Flory–Huggins-style polymer–solute interactions
  • PAH solubilization and formulation-comparison concepts

Research record

Related conference work: encapsulation of polyaromatic hydrocarbons in block copolymers, presented in the undergraduate research period.
