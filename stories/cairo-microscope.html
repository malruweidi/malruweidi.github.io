A microscope of my own

The microscope I brought home from Cairo.

I bought this microscope in the Qasr El Einy area of Cairo, a city close to my roots, and brought it back to Qatar despite the trouble of transporting it. I wanted an instrument of my own, one I could return to whenever a question occurred to me, without waiting for the next scheduled laboratory session.

In the photograph, the microscope is standing in my room, among books, notes and an American Chemical Society mug. It was a vacation morning, not an assigned laboratory session. There was no experiment to finish for a supervisor and no result to present. I liked having the possibility of observing something simply because it interested me, and of spending as long as I wanted trying to understand what I saw.

That sort of freedom mattered to my education. At university, instruments came with protocols, schedules and good reasons for both. At home, the microscope gave my curiosity somewhere to go between those structured encounters. I could follow my own interests, return to an observation and learn to notice the difference between seeing something and knowing what it meant.

That instrument gave me practice I could choose for myself: looking carefully, coming back to the same observation, and learning how much more work lay between seeing something and explaining it. Proper research would still demand controls, reproducible methods and explanations that survived criticism. When I look at the photograph now, I see a piece of Cairo that travelled with me to Qatar, and an ordinary morning on which science belonged to my own time.

From the record

The photograph comes from my personal archive, originally posted on 1 July 2017. Buying the microscope in Cairo's Qasr El Einy area and bringing it to Qatar are my firsthand recollections; the photograph documents the instrument at home, not the journey or any particular scientific finding.
