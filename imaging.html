QU Biomedical Research Center / experimental infrastructure

Making biological
phenomena measurable.

An imaging unit becomes a scientific capability when its equipment, methods and people can turn biological motion into reproducible measurements. At Qatar University’s Biomedical Research Center, Al-Ruweidi contributed to establishing and developing imaging workflows for zebrafish cardiovascular research, developmental biology and experimental injury models.

Research infrastructure case study

From specimen preparation to interpretable data.

The work connected specimen preparation and optical acquisition with quantitative analysis, method development and laboratory coordination. The practical objective was to make imaging usable across biological, engineering and computational research questions rather than dependent on a single investigator or experiment.

Cardiac motion, vascular flow and morphological changes become evidence only when acquisition settings, temporal resolution and measurement definitions can be examined and reproduced. The infrastructure matters because the science depends upon it.

The imaging workflow

01 · MODEL

Prepare the biological system.

Zebrafish specimens and experimental protocols relevant to embryonic development, cardiac mechanics and vessel analysis.

02 · ACQUIRE

Capture dynamics, not just still frames.

High-speed microscopy for biological processes that require temporal information, including contraction and blood-flow assessment.

03 · QUANTIFY

Transform images into measurements.

ImageJ and MicroZebraLab workflows, geometric measurements, interpretation and collaboration with biologists and engineering researchers.

04 · STANDARDIZE

Make the method usable by others.

Protocol development, experimental preparation, reproducibility and workflow documentation within the BRC research environment.
